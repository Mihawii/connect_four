# Inferno — Product Context

**Register:** product (the app UI serves the game), with a brand-leaning marketing home page.

## Product purpose

Inferno is blitz Connect Four with a burning board. The one new rule: **every disc lasts exactly 10 of its player's own turns, then it burns away and pieces above fall by gravity.** Wins still count at the moment of placement. Three formats: Classic (no decay), Inferno (decay, untimed), Inferno Blitz (decay + 60s bullet clock). Connect Four is a solved, commoditized genre; the decay rule + blitz identity + a premium 3D board + an AI coach is the wedge that makes it feel new.

## Users

- **Primary:** 16–30, casual-competitive, Twitch/Discord/TikTok-native. Plays Wordle daily, has a Chess.com or Lichess account they touch sometimes, would clip a clean win to a story. Plays in 90-second bursts on a phone or a laptop on the couch.
- **Secondary:** parents who want a short strategic brain workout for a kid (the Learn tab).

## Brand & tone

Confident, a little dangerous, never tryhard. The fire is an *accent and an idea*, not a wash. The product should feel like a premium indie toy or an editorial zine about a game, not a hacker terminal and not a generic SaaS dashboard. Playful but sharp. Big type, real opinions, lots of air.

## Strategic principles

- **No ads, ever.** Counter-positioning against every ad-cluttered competitor (papergames, foony, CrazyGames). Funded by Pro ($3.99/mo) + skins + battle pass.
- **No dark patterns.** Generous free tier, one-click cancel.
- **The 3D board is the moat.** Every competitor is flat 2D. Our board must look like a physical object you want to touch. The burn moment is the shareable hook.
- **Accessible by default.** Red/yellow is the worst possible colorblind pairing; discs must carry a second cue (shape/pattern), not rely on hue.
- **Speed is the feeling.** Matches are short and the UI should feel instant and physical, never sluggish.

## Anti-references (what to NOT look like)

- The muddy dark-brown-on-black "fire game" cliché (this was the rejected v1).
- Decorative gradients of any kind. Banned.
- Generic AI-default icon sets (lucide/heroicons used flat). Use a deliberate, characterful icon set instead.
- SaaS hero-metric template, identical card grids, glassmorphism.
- Neon-on-black "crypto/gamer" energy.

## Reference aesthetic the user endorsed

A GSAP hero loader in a warm editorial / risograph style: bone/cream paper, heavy grotesk display type, bold ink-black line work, one bold pop color, a spinning circular badge, a playful falling-token mouse trail. This is the north star for the visual language.
